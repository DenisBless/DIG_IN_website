Overview

Sampling from a discrete distribution known only up to its normalizing constant is hard. MCMC mixes slowly across energy barriers, and existing discrete neural samplers tend to collapse onto a single mode. DIG-IN trains a masked diffusion sampler from density evaluations alone, with no training data.

Each iteration draws configurations from the current model, tempers their importance weights to a target effective sample size, and fits the model with a weighted cross-entropy. The labels in that loss are Gibbs labels: the normalized distribution over single-site changes of a configuration, a discrete analogue of the score. They give a strong learning signal and keep the sampler from collapsing onto one mode.

  • Ising & PottsThe only method in our comparison that covers every mode in the ordered phase while also giving accurate ln Z.
  • AmortizedOne sampler conditioned on (β, h), on lattices up to D = 2500 sites.
  • AlloysRecovers the phase diagrams of Ag–Pd and Cu–Au from a single sampler conditioned on (T, Δμ).
  • PeptidesA Boltzmann generator on binned coordinates of alanine dipeptide and tetrapeptide, trained from the energy alone, with no molecular-dynamics data.
  • Free energiesA learned bridge recovers the solvation free energy of alanine dipeptide to within 0.01 of BAR on the molecular dynamics of both states, from samples of one state and the energy of the other.
  • RLAs a binned policy for continuous control, the highest final return among the Gaussian, diffusion and flow policies we compare against on 20 DeepMind Control tasks.

Alloy phase diagrams

For Ag–Pd and Cu–Au, a single sampler is conditioned on temperature T and chemical-potential difference Δμ over a DFT-fitted cluster expansion. Its free-energy estimates give the phase diagram through a common-tangent construction. Discretized Boltzmann generators

Boltzmann generators sample the equilibrium distribution of a molecule with a generative model of exact likelihood, so that importance weights remove the remaining bias. We cut every coordinate of alanine dipeptide (22 atoms) and alanine tetrapeptide (42 atoms) into bins, in internal coordinates, and train DIG-IN from the potential energy alone. A uniform point inside each bin makes a draw continuous with an exact density. The film replays draws of the trained models network call by network call and compares two million draws per run with long molecular dynamics.

Free energy estimation

DIG-IN's bridge learns a pair of time-reversed CTMCs between two Boltzmann distributions, from samples of the first and the energy of the second. On FEAT's alanine dipeptide tasks it carries molecular-dynamics frames from vacuum into implicit solvent (ALDP-S), and from one conformational state across a barrier into another (ALDP-T). The weights of the paths give the free-energy difference.

Maximum entropy reinforcement learning

In maximum entropy reinforcement learning, the improved policy at every state s is the Boltzmann distribution of the soft action value, π(a | s) ∝ exp(Q(s, a)/τ). Learning the policy is thus an amortized sampling problem over states, whose energy is a learned critic. Most policies for continuous control represent the action directly, as a Gaussian, diffusion or flow model. We instead cut every action dimension into 64 bins and use DIG-IN's masked denoiser, conditioned on the state, as the policy, trained on Gibbs labels read from the critic.
